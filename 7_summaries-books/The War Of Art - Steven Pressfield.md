# The War Of Art - Steven Pressfield

Resistance is a force within that stops us anytime we have to do any beneficial long-term activity.
Career pursuits, launching of businesses, diet, overcoming addiction, education / learning, long term commitments.

## how willpower works

The more you do hard actions you don't want to do that put you towards your goals, the stronger your willpower becomes.
This is temporary however, it takes a longer time, but willpower deteriorates. It's important to do hard actions in order to maintain willpower.
There is a limited amount of willpower.
It's like a liquid that increases in amount each time you do actions you don't want to do, and decreases each time you give in to temptation.
With the amount being based on how hard and important to our evolution those actions were.
If you do some action repeatedly, even if it's hard, it will turn into a habit, which will decrease friction and make you spend less willpower on it.
Another way to decrease the willpower needed is `friction engineering`.
Tidy up your environment, make actions you need to do accessible and make negative actions harder to access.
This way, less willpower is spent on actions you need to do and more is spent on actions you **don't** want to do.

## amateurs wait for inspiration

> “Inspiration is for amateurs. The rest of us just show up and get to work.
> If you wait around for the clouds to part and a bolt of lightning to strike you in the brain,
> you are not going to make an awful lot of work. All the best ideas come out of the process; they come out of the work itself.”
> - Chuck Close

> "Don't break the chain."
> - Jerry Seinfeld

You have to work, until it turns into a habit.
Not breaking the chain, the habit of working, is very important.
You have to force yourself to do projects, even if they will be low quality, because that trains the habit of working, which in turn will eventually turn quantity into quality.

## resistance could be used as a map

Rule of thumb: the more important an action is to our evolution, the more Resistance we will feel toward pursuing it.
- Resistance is universal. Everyone who has a body experiences it
- the danger of Resistance is greatest when the finish line is in sight
- procrastination is the most common manifestation of Resistance, when procrastinating, we don't tell ourselves that we will never do what must be done, we come up with excuses and delay execution
- the worst part of procrastination is that it could become a habit
- rationalization is the right hand man of Resistance.
  Rationalizations sound legit, but they are actually you lying to yourself
- consumerism exploits feelings of procrastination and rationalizes consumption of their products as an antidote to emptiness and Resistance
- Resistance uses fear to break your will

## amateurs

- only amateurs over-identify with their professions.
  Musician, programmer, writer...
  When you over-identify with your profession, Resistance in creating anything in that profession increases, now you can't fail or experiment with a bunch of projects, you've got an ego to maintain, you become too invested

## professionals

- a professional doesn't wait for inspiration. He shows up every day, sits down and starts working, which activates Flow State, which after repeated execution turns into a habit
- a professional understands long-term gratification
- a professional hates disorder. He eliminates chaos to lower friction for important tasks
- a professional acts in the face of fear, which gives him discipline, while an amateur lets fear consume him as he starts procrastinating and making excuses
- a professional understands that if he caves in to Resistance, no matter the excuse, he will be twice as likely to cave in tomorrow
- a professional doesn't take humiliation and criticism personally
- a professional recognizes his limitations and fills his weaknesses with other people's strengths, for example: hiring a lawyer, accountant, etc
